I am a Ph.D. student in Political Science at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, working at the intersection of comparative politics and computational social science.

About

My background spans regional studies and data science, and my research centers on contentious politics and ethnic politics in the Middle East and North Africa. I am especially interested in political behavior, state responses to dissent, and how religion and identity are mobilized in political discourse.

Methodologically, I combine natural language processing, causal inference, and comparative analysis with sustained fieldwork and close attention to regional context. Before Madison, I earned an M.S. in Information (Data Science & Big Data Analytics) and an M.A. in International & Regional Studies (MENA) at the University of Michigan, and a B.A. in Modern Hebrew at Beijing Foreign Studies University.
